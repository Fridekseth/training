## What

The tone of voice in learning is about how all elements speak to the user. It is short, direct and practical, but a bit warmer than the rest of the interface. It speaks to the user as "you", and explains both what something is and what to do with it.

## Why

The users are experienced professionals, learning in their own workplace, often with little time. The text needs to respect that: get to the point, use the words they use on board, and never talk down to them.

At the same time, learning can afford to be more human than the operational interface. Like in the illustrations, a calmer and more approachable tone helps separate learning from the live system, and lowers the threshold to try, fail and try again.

## How to use

- Keep it short. One or two sentences per step, so it can be read at a glance.
- Start with what the element does, then say why it matters. For example: "Compare this bar to its STBD counterpart, to notice any irregularities during deployment."
- Set expectations. Say how long something takes, and how a scenario ends: "Deployment takes about 2-3 minutes."
- Use the same names as the interface and the crew: deploy, retract, STBD, PORT.
- Give feedback plainly. "Correct" and "Incorrect" are enough, followed by what to look at next.
- Leave out jokes and exclamation marks in the instructions. The warmth comes from being clear and helpful.
