Having done a visual exploration and research of the state of the art within learning, I began to
structure my findings into a starting framework for in-system learning. Each part of it is a
question that a training solution has to answer:

- **Type:** What types of training can we provide?
- **Time and place:** When and where does the training happen?
- **Target group:** Is it for the individual, or for the team?
- **Boundaries, standards and laws:** What are the technical boundaries training must follow?
- **Visual communication:** How is training communicated?
- **Goal:** What do we wish to accomplish?
- **Evaluation:** How do we know that the training is successful?

For my early prototyping and user testing, I focused on exploring the first three points: the
type of training, the time and place it happens, and who it is for.

## What types of training are there?

The word training covers a lot of different situations, so I started by sorting them into four
types.

### Initial training

The training you receive when first entering a new workplace: the initial onboarding, and the
courses one must pass in order to operate the system independently. Refresher courses could also
fall under this category.

### Further development

There is also a need to develop more advanced skills, going from proficient to professional user.
This could be scenario based learning, for situations that are rare or critical and so are hard to
practice in real operation, and free exploring of the interface, where the user can try things
out without consequences.

### Coaching

Training that is driven through personal feedback on performance. It could give the user:

- Personal feedback on how they operate the system
- An overview of their personal development and progress

### In-action support

Help that is there while the system is being used, such as a quick help, like a "first aid kit",
for the moment something goes wrong or is unclear.

## Time and place

The next question is when and where the training happens. I found three main options.

### A separate training menu, for when the system is not running

The training is kept apart from the interface itself, and is used when it is safe to step away
from operating. It could be reached through:

- A pop-up modal
- A settings page
- Its own menu in the corner

It would be used before using the interface, or during breaks and in non-critical states.

### Within the interface, in real time

The training is a part of the interface, and is there in the context of the action it explains:

- Tooltips or toggletips on complex components
- In the header
- In the action context

### Outside the system

The training is not on the screen of the system at all:

- An AR overlay
- A second screen

## Target group

The last of the three points is who the training is for. It can be aimed at the individual, for
example a personal training log, or at the team, where several people on the same vessel, or on
different vessels, share what they have learned. The two do not exclude each other, and I wanted
to keep both open in the prototypes.

In the [first prototyping experiments](/blog/prototyping-experiments) I started trying out some of
these ideas in code.
