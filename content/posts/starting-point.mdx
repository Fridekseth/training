Bridge interfaces are learned in three places, none of them the interface
itself: a simulator, a manual, or a colleague standing over your shoulder. Each
works, and each has the same weakness — the knowledge lives somewhere other
than where the work happens.

OpenBridge makes a strong case that consistency across vendors is a design
problem worth solving centrally. My question for this course is whether
*learnability* deserves the same treatment.

## What I want to find out

- Where operators currently get stuck, and whether those moments are
  predictable enough to design for.
- What a design system can legitimately specify about learning, as opposed to
  what has to be left to training programmes.
- Which existing patterns — onboarding, progressive disclosure, guided modes —
  survive contact with a safety-critical bridge context, and which do not.
